# Every Position Is a Station: Your 2026 Draft Guide

*By GH | thegavfather.com*

---

If you have watched The Bear you know 
the kitchen runs on timing.

Every station has a role. Every ingredient 
has a moment. Pull the duck too early and 
it is raw. Wait too long and it is ruined. 
The best chefs know exactly when to act.

Fantasy drafts work the same way.

Draft a QB in round 1 of a standard league 
and you have wasted a pick. Draft a TE 
in round 12 and you are streaming all season. 
The Gavfather System tells you when each 
position reaches its tipping point — and 
what to do before and after it.

---

## The QB Station

**Standard leagues: rounds 7-10**

There are 32 starting QBs. You need one. 
The replacement level QB — the 13th best 
you could pick up off waivers — projects 
around 14-15 PPG. The gap between QB1 
and QB13 is real but not as large as 
the gap between RB1 and RB13.

Wait. Let the rest of the table panic-reach 
for QBs in rounds 4-6. Take your pick 
from the second tier when the value is right.

**Exception:** Josh Allen in standard is 
worth reaching slightly. His rushing floor 
makes him genuinely different from every 
other QB. If he falls to round 2 late, take him.

**Superflex leagues: rounds 1-3**

Everything changes. You need two startable QBs. 
The replacement level is the 25th best QB 
which is genuinely bad. 

Allen, Lamar, Mahomes, Hurts — these go 
in the first three rounds in superflex. 
Missing the run means starting a streaming 
QB every week. Do not miss the run.

---

## The RB Station

**All formats: rounds 1-5 and rounds 9-12**

RB is an hourglass position. The elite tier 
(Gibbs, Bijan, Achane, Taylor, Williams, Cook) 
is genuinely scarce. Miss on all of them and 
you are assembling a committee of middling backs.

The model's RB tiers for 2026:

Tier 1 (rounds 1-2): Gibbs, Bijan, Achane, JT
Tier 2 (rounds 3-4): Kyren Williams, James Cook, 
  De'Von Achane if available
Tier 3 (rounds 5-7): CMC, Jacobs, Henry, 
  Skattebo, Jeanty
Tier 4 (rounds 8-11): Charbonnet, Tracy, 
  Chase Brown, Breece Hall

**The committee warning:** When two backs 
split carries between 30-50% each, neither 
is reliably startable. The model calculates 
a committee risk score for every backfield. 
Avoid confirmed committees even in late rounds.

## The WR Station

**All formats: everywhere, but tier-aware**

WR is the deepest position in the draft. 
There are legitimate WR3 values available 
in rounds 10-12. This means you can 
afford to be patient early.

The model's WR tiers for 2026:

Tier 1 (rounds 1-2): Nacua, Chase, Amon-Ra
Tier 2 (rounds 3-4): CeeDee Lamb, JSN, Collins
Tier 3 (rounds 5-7): Higgins, AJ Brown, 
  Drake London, Rashee Rice
Tier 4 (rounds 8-10): DK Metcalf, Sutton, 
  Brian Thomas, Jordan Addison
Tier 5 (value): Jauan Jennings, Romeo Doubs, 
  Kyren Williams receiving role

**International game flag:** AJ Brown (London Wk6), 
Nico Collins (London Wk7), and Puka Nacua 
(Melbourne Wk1) all have at least one 
international game. Score accordingly on 
those specific weeks.

## The TE Station

**This is the most debated station in the kitchen.**

Here is the honest answer from the data.

The gap between TE1 (McBride, Kittle, Bowers) 
and TE13 (streaming option) is enormous. 
Larger than any other position. A great TE 
gives you a genuine weekly advantage. 
A streaming TE gives you nothing.

But the top TEs cost draft capital that 
could go elsewhere.

The Gavfather System's recommendation:

**If McBride, Kittle, or Bowers are available 
in rounds 3-4:** Take one. The positional 
advantage is worth the early pick.

**If you miss all three:** Wait until rounds 
7-9 for the second tier (Goedert, Kelce, 
Fannin, Warren, Loveland). Do not reach 
in rounds 5-6 for a TE4.

**Never stream TE if you can avoid it.**
The variance is too high and the floor 
is too low. One bad matchup wrecks your week.

**49ers TE note:** Kittle is TE2 in our rankings 
but plays two international games (Melbourne Wk1, 
Mexico City Wk11). Know those weeks coming in.

## Putting It Together

The Bear kitchen works because everyone 
knows their station and their timing.

Your draft kitchen:

Rounds 1-3: Best available RB or WR. 
In superflex grab your first QB here.

Rounds 4-6: Fill the positions you missed. 
TE if McBride/Kittle are gone. Second RB 
or WR otherwise.

Rounds 7-9: QB in standard. Second TE 
or RB depth.

Rounds 10-12: Value hunting. 
BUY signals from the model. 
Upside plays at WR and RB.

Rounds 13+: Handcuffs, rookies with paths 
to playing time, and one dart throw.
