# The 10 People at Every Fantasy Draft

*By GH | thegavfather.com*

Every fantasy draft has the same cast of characters.

Different names. Different cities. Different snack spreads. Same people.

The Gavfather System analyzed 22 years of NFL play-by-play data to find out which of these people is actually right.

Spoiler: most of them aren't.

## 1. The Podcast Listener

*"I'm taking him. Rotoworld, Fantasy Footballers, FantasyPros, the Athletic, and my neighbor's cousin who has a Substack all said so."*

This person has consumed approximately 847 hours of fantasy football content since March. They can tell you the consensus ADP of every player to two decimal places. They have never once questioned whether the consensus is right.

**What the data says:**

Third down target share — the stat every podcast uses to identify the QB's security blanket — actually predicts LOWER future production.

The Gavfather System found this independently across 22 years of data. The correlation is negative. Statistically significant. Consistent across all four eras of NFL play from 2004 through 2025.

The consensus is built on the same metrics everyone uses. If you only know what everyone knows you can only finish where everyone finishes.

The Podcast Listener finishes 6th. Every year.

## 2. The Veteran Who Won't Let Go

*"I know he's 30 but this is the year he bounces back. He looked great in that one preseason clip."*

This person has been burned by Marshawn Lynch, LeSean McCoy, Adrian Peterson, and Todd Gurley. They will be burned again. They cannot help themselves. The heart wants what it wants.

**What the data says:**

The RB age cliff is real, steep, and merciless.

Age 28: -8% projected PPG
Age 29: -15% projected PPG
Age 30+: -25% projected PPG

The Gavfather System applies these penalties automatically. The Veteran Who Won't Let Go applies them never.

Derrick Henry is 32. The model knows. The Veteran does not want to know.

## 3. The Obscure Stats Guy

*"I'm taking him RB1 because his yards after contact on third down in cold weather road games against cover-2 defenses improved 11% last year."*

Nobody at the table knows what he's talking about. He might be a genius. He might be making it up. The line between advanced analytics and seafood-based scouting reports is thinner than you think.

**What the data says:**

Here's the irony. The single most predictive signal in 22 years of NFL data — the one metric that beats target share, touchdowns, snap counts, and everything else — is something nobody talks about on any podcast.

The Boom-Consistency Index. Players who score big regularly AND never completely disappear.

Nobody has a segment on it. Nobody debates it. The Obscure Stats Guy would love it.

He is still probably wrong about the cold weather road game thing.

## 4. The One Who Drafted Before Camp Ended

*"I did my research in June. I'm locked in."*

This person printed their cheat sheet in July, laminated it, and has not looked at football news since. They are unaware that 21 of 32 NFL teams changed offensive coordinators this offseason. They are unaware that Mahomes tore his ACL. They are unaware of approximately everything that happened in the last six weeks.

**What the data says:**

21 of 32 NFL teams have a new offensive coordinator in 2026. The most coordinator turnover in NFL history.

Every ranking from before those hires is wrong to some degree. Mike McDaniel left his HEAD COACHING JOB to become Herbert's offensive coordinator. That happened in February.

The laminated cheat sheet does not reflect this.

The One Who Drafted Before Camp Ended will spend Week 1 wondering why their players look different.

## 5. The Revenge Drafter

*"I'm not taking him. He cost me the championship last year."*

This person is making roster decisions based on a single game from eleven months ago. They have a list. Certain players are on the list. The list cannot be questioned.

**What the data says:**

Garbage time inflation is real.

The Gavfather System strips out all plays where win probability drops below 20% — the drives where teams are down three scores and just throwing to run up stats.

A player who torched the Revenge Drafter's defense for 140 yards and 2 TDs in garbage time last December is not necessarily a good fantasy player. They were probably down 35-7 facing a prevent defense.

The Revenge Drafter's nemesis may have been a fraud the whole time.

This will not make the Revenge Drafter feel better about it.

## 6. The Handcuff Hoarder

*"I'm taking his handcuff. And his handcuff's handcuff. Just in case."*

By round 10 this person has four running backs who will never see the field unless a starter, a backup, and an emergency third-stringer all go down in the same week. Their bench is a museum of optimism.

**What the data says:**

The committee RB trap is one of the most common ways to waste draft capital.

When two backs split carries between 30-50% each — what the model flags as a true committee — neither is reliably startable.

Hoarding handcuffs behind committee backs is hoarding nothing. You need the handcuff behind a bellcow to get real value.

The Handcuff Hoarder has four committees and zero bellcows.

## 7. The Rookie Reacher

*"He is going to be special. I can feel it."*

Every year this person takes a rookie two rounds before anyone else. Every year they explain patiently that the college production, combine numbers, and perceived opportunity make him a lock.

Every year it does not work out. Next year will be different.

**What the data says:**

The Gavfather System's bust prediction model is 86% accurate. The breakout prediction model — the one that finds the Rookie Reacher's guy — is 63% accurate.

Breakouts are genuinely hard to predict. Even the machine struggles.

The market has Tetairoa McMillan going as the 16th wide receiver off the board. The Gavfather has him WR48.

One of us is right. One of us is working with feelings.

## 8. The Homer

*"I'm taking him. He plays for my team."*

Logic, data, and the laws of supply and demand do not apply to the Homer. Their city's players are always undervalued. Their city's players always have upside. Their city's players are always due for a breakout.

**What the data says:**

Bad teams trail in games. A lot.

When teams trail by more than two scores offenses abandon the run and throw constantly. This inflates stats in ways that look great on paper but do not win fantasy matchups.

The Gavfather System applies a game script adjustment for every player based on their team's projected win total.

Players on projected bad teams get a discount. Not because the model hates them. Because trailing by 21 points in the fourth quarter is not a sustainable fantasy strategy.

If the Homer's team was good the Homer would just be The Sniper.

The Homer's team is not good.

## 9. The Star Loyalist

*"He is the best player in football. I do not care what anyone says."*

This person is not wrong that their guy is great. They are wrong that greatness is permanent, injury-proof, and scheme-independent. They will not be told otherwise. They have a flag.

**What the data says:**

The market has Dak Prescott as the 5th quarterback off the board.

The Gavfather System has him QB20.

That is a fifteen-spot positional disagreement based on real data. Injury history. Dallas situation score of 49.7 out of 100. A receiving corps that lost its best player.

The market remembers Dak's good years. The model only knows what the numbers say now.

The Star Loyalist is going to be very confident about this pick.

## 10. The Sniper

*Picks 1 through 47 happen.*

*Pick 48.*

*"...I'll take Herbert."*

Dead silence.

Then: "Are you SERIOUS."
And: "I had him circled since round 3."
And: "How did you KNOW."

The Sniper says nothing. The Sniper just smiles.

This person has been watching your face all draft. They know who everyone wants. They know whose name is on whose cheat sheet. And one pick before you were going to take your guy — they take him.

It does not feel like a reach to them. It feels exactly right.

**What the data says:**

The Gavfather System has Justin Herbert ranked QB3. The market drafts him QB11.

Eight spots. At the position. That is the largest QB disagreement between our model and consensus ADP right now.

The model says: Mike McDaniel left his head coaching job to become Herbert's offensive coordinator. Herbert called the new scheme "an oasis in the desert." The system applies plus 9 percent for the upgrade. The situation is elite. The talent was always there.

The market says: round 6 or 7, maybe later.

The Sniper does not wait for round 6 or 7. The Sniper saw the gap. The Sniper trusted the gap. The Sniper took him at pick 48 while you were deciding between your fourth RB and your third WR.

You had Herbert circled. You thought he would slip.

He did not.

Be the Sniper.
